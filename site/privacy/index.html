Privacy

Privacy Policy

Effective date: REPLACE-ME

This policy covers Kakureneko for Mac, Kakureneko for iPhone and this website. It says what each one stores, where, for how long, and who can see it.

The short version

  • There is no Kakureneko account and no server of our own. Your Mac and your iPhone talk through Apple's iCloud.
  • What goes through iCloud: alerts, your Mac's status, the requests your iPhone sends (button taps, and a status refresh when you open the app), and a small record for each iPhone. Alerts are deleted after three days.
  • Other iCloud users can't read your records. They are not end-to-end encrypted, though, and as the developer we can technically see them through Apple's tools.
  • No analytics, no tracking, no ads. The only third-party code is Sparkle, which the Mac app uses to check for updates.
  • Everything else stays on your Mac.

Who we are

Kakureneko is made by REPLACE-ME. We decide how the data described here is used, and we're the people to contact about it.

We don't sell, rent or share your data, and we don't use it for advertising. The only other parties involved are Apple, which hosts iCloud and delivers the notifications, and the company that hosts this website and the Mac app's updates.

On your Mac

Kakureneko for Mac runs on your Mac and keeps its data there:

  • Settings, the alerts waiting to be sent, and the list of your iPhones, in ~/Library/Application Support/Kakureneko.
  • Logs, in ~/.kakureneko/log. They record what Kakureneko decided (for example that it kept the Mac awake or slowed an app down), not your prompts or the agents' messages.
  • What each agent session is doing right now, and backups of agent settings it changed, in ~/.kakureneko.

To know what an agent is doing, Kakureneko reads the events its hook writes and the agent's own session files on your Mac. To decide which apps to slow down, it looks at the apps running on your Mac. None of that leaves the Mac, except what goes into an alert or the Mac's status, described below.

Kakureneko changes an agent's configuration files only when you ask it to. It shows you the exact change first and backs up the file before writing.

Between your Mac and iPhone

The Mac app and the iPhone app exchange a few records through CloudKit, Apple's iCloud database service. There is no server of ours in between.

Where the records are stored

The records are stored at Apple, in Kakureneko's public CloudKit database. "Public" is Apple's name for the database that belongs to the app rather than to your personal iCloud. It does not mean anyone can read it:

  • Other iCloud users can't. Each record can be read and changed only by the Apple Account that created it. Your Macs and iPhones signed in to your Apple Account can read your records; nobody else's devices can.
  • It doesn't use your iCloud storage. The space comes from Kakureneko's allowance at Apple, not from your plan.
  • It isn't end-to-end encrypted. Data travels encrypted between your devices and iCloud, but Kakureneko doesn't add encryption of its own. Apple hosts the data under Apple's privacy policy.
  • We can access it. As the developer, we can open Kakureneko's public database with Apple's CloudKit tools. We don't use the data for anything except running Kakureneko, and we only look at it to fix a problem you report to us.

The records are tied to a random identifier that iCloud gives your Apple Account for Kakureneko. We can't see your name or email address from it. Your Mac's name is included, though, and it often contains a first name (for example "Alex's MacBook Pro"). You can change it in System Settings → General → Sharing.

When iCloud is used

Kakureneko for Mac writes to iCloud only while "Send alerts to your iPhone" is on (Settings → Notifications; it's on by default) and the Mac is signed in to iCloud. Then it keeps the Mac's status record up to date and writes alerts when their timing allows, by default only while the lid is closed. Turning that setting off deletes the Mac's status record and the alerts it saved, and nothing more is written until you turn it on again.

Kakureneko for iPhone uses iCloud as soon as you open it on an iPhone that's signed in to iCloud.

Alerts reach your iPhone through Apple's push notification service. The iPhone app keeps a copy of the last three days of alerts and your Macs' latest status on the iPhone itself. Deleting the app deletes that copy.

What an alert says

You choose how much an alert says in Kakureneko for Mac, under Settings → Notifications → Detail. The same text appears on your Mac and your iPhone.

Alert text at each detail level
Detail levelAlert titles and text contain
Private No agent or project names: "An agent finished", "An agent needs your input", with counts and times.
Basic (default) The agent's name, the project folder's name, the app it runs in (for example Terminal) and how long it worked. When an agent waits for you, also what it's waiting for, in up to 140 characters: the permission it asks for, or its question.
Detailed Everything in Basic, plus up to 140 characters of the agent's last message.

Before any text from an agent leaves your Mac, Kakureneko removes things that look like API keys, access tokens and private keys, and shortens file paths to the file name. This is a safety net, not a guarantee: if your agents handle sensitive text, choose Private.

The detail level also applies to the Mac status: at Private it lists only which agents are running and what they're doing, without project folder or app names. At Basic and Detailed it includes them.

How long it's kept

  • Alerts: three days. Your Macs, and the iPhone app once a day, delete alerts older than three and a half days, whichever Mac sent them, so they don't stay behind when a Mac is gone.
  • Commands: until the Mac reads them, normally seconds. One no Mac read is deleted after an hour.
  • Mac status: one per Mac, replaced on every update. Deleted when you turn off "Send alerts to your iPhone", uninstall Kakureneko, tap Remove on the iPhone, or after 30 days without an update.
  • iPhone records: one per iPhone, kept when you reinstall the app. Deleted when you tap Remove on the Mac (Settings → Notifications) or after 30 days without an update.
  • On your Mac and iPhone: until you delete Kakureneko. The iPhone app's copy of alerts only covers the last three days.

Updates for the Mac app

Kakureneko for Mac uses Sparkle, an open-source updater, to check for new versions. When it checks, it downloads a small update feed from our website, and new versions come from our website too. Like any web request, this shows our website's host your IP address. The request also names the version of Kakureneko you have. Sparkle can send an optional system profile (hardware and macOS details); Kakureneko leaves it turned off and adds nothing to the request, so it isn't sent.

This website

This website uses no cookies, no analytics and no scripts. The company that hosts it may keep standard server logs, such as IP addresses, browser type, the pages requested and the time, to run and protect the site.

Crash reports

Neither app has a crash reporter of its own.

  • iPhone: if you turned on "Share With App Developers" (Settings → Privacy & Security → Analytics & Improvements), Apple shares crash reports and usage statistics for the iPhone app with us. You can turn that off there at any time.
  • Mac: Kakureneko sends no crash reports to us. macOS may send them to Apple if you share Mac analytics with Apple, under Apple's privacy policy. If something goes wrong, you can send us a report or a log yourself.

Permissions

  • Notifications (Mac and iPhone): to show alerts. On iPhone, Time Sensitive alerts let the important ones, such as "an agent needs you", through Focus.
  • iCloud (Mac and iPhone): to exchange the records above. Kakureneko uses the Apple Account your devices are already signed in to.
  • Administrator approval, once (Mac): to install closed-lid mode. See Support for exactly what it installs.
  • Automation (Mac): only if you choose "Shut down" when agents are done, so Kakureneko can ask macOS to shut down.

Your choices

  • Say less: choose the Private detail level in Kakureneko for Mac (Settings → Notifications → Detail).
  • Stop iPhone alerts: turn off "Send alerts to your iPhone" in Settings → Notifications. The Mac's status and the alerts it saved are deleted from iCloud, and nothing more is written there.
  • Remove the iPhone app: it stops writing to iCloud. Its iPhone record is deleted after 30 days without an update by any of your devices still running Kakureneko, or at once when you tap Remove on the Mac (Settings → Notifications).
  • Remove Kakureneko from your Mac: Settings → General → Uninstall Kakureneko… removes Kakureneko and everything it put on your Mac, and first deletes the Mac's status and its alerts from iCloud. If the Mac is offline at that moment, they stay; write to us and we'll delete them.
  • Ask us: you can ask what we hold about you, and ask us to delete your iCloud records. Write to the address below.

Children

Kakureneko is a tool for software developers. It isn't directed at children, and we don't knowingly collect data from children under 13, or the minimum age where you live.
